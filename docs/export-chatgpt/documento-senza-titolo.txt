CUSTOM ANALOGUE DJ / DUB MIXER
Project status & engineering study
Status date: 18 September 2026
Purpose
This document consolidates the project as currently defined. It separates:
	•	LOCKED — functional/design decisions established by the project.
	•	PROPOSED — useful engineering starting points, not yet frozen.
	•	OPEN — requires further technical or creative decisions.
	•	SUPERSEDED — earlier ideas that have been replaced and should not remain active specifications.
This is not yet a production-ready schematic, PCB design or BOM. The overall architecture is considered feasible, but several circuit blocks still require proper topology selection, mathematical derivation, simulation, prototyping and measurement.

1. EXECUTIVE STATUS
The overall system is technically feasible as a high-end custom analogue DJ / sound-system mixer.
There is no major subsystem identified so far that is fundamentally impossible.
The project has reached an important transition point:
Previous phase: functional requirements → plausible analogue concepts → provisional numerical values
Next phase: functional requirement → transfer function → circuit topology → component selection → simulation → PCB/layout → prototype → measurement → revision
The most technically demanding sections are:
	•	the global DJ filter,
	•	its resonance/self-oscillation system,
	•	the five-way KILL/filter-bank architecture,
	•	the five-band creative EQ,
	•	analogue control-voltage distribution,
	•	the complete noise budget,
	•	the power/ground architecture,
	•	the modular backplane.

2. GLOBAL PROJECT PRINCIPLES — LOCKED
Audio
	•	The complete audio path is 100% analogue.
	•	No ADC, DAC, DSP or digital audio processing.
	•	Stereo master L/R.
	•	The mixer must remain stereo-capable even though it may often feed a mono sound system.
	•	No pan controls.
	•	No separate user-controlled L/R controls.
	•	Stereo controls are linked.
Modular architecture
	•	Functional modules/cards.
	•	Serviceable and replaceable.
	•	Backplane-based architecture.
	•	Inspired by Eurorack conceptually, but not required to follow the Eurorack electrical standard.
	•	No direct access from one module to another module's internal analogue nodes.
	•	Modules communicate through defined audio/control/power interfaces.
	•	No hot swapping.
Performance philosophy
	•	Extreme settings are intentionally allowed.
	•	Filter resonance may self-oscillate.
	•	Filter and 5-way stages may deliberately overload.
	•	Cross-FX feedback loops are allowed.
	•	These behaviours must remain electrically non-destructive.
Dynamics
	•	No intentional master limiter.
	•	No master compressor.
	•	No hidden automatic gain control.
	•	Protection circuitry is required, but this is electrical protection rather than audio dynamics processing.
	•	Fast overload recovery is desirable throughout the analogue path.

11. FILTER RESONANCE
Proposed control behaviour
	•	0–70%: normal musical resonance.
	•	70–90%: aggressive / near oscillation.
	•	90–100%: self-oscillation.
The oscillation should approximately follow the selected cutoff.
Proposed internal oscillation level
Approximately:
+6 to +10 dBu maximum target
The intent is to use soft nonlinear control of the feedback/output rather than a crude hard diode clamp.
Major engineering tasks
The actual circuit must guarantee:
	•	stable normal operation,
	•	predictable transition to oscillation,
	•	bounded oscillation amplitude,
	•	acceptable behaviour at 20 Hz,
	•	acceptable behaviour at 20 kHz,
	•	no unwanted parasitic oscillation,
	•	musical overload behaviour.
This is one of the most technically demanding circuits in the project.

12. FILTER SWITCHING
The individual channel filter cores remain operational, while the channel chooses:
	•	HPF,
	•	OFF,
	•	LPF.
Switching should use:
	•	break-before-make,
	•	approximately 1–5 ms click suppression/ramp.
Goal:
no audible switching click while changing filter mode live.

34. IMPORTANT ENGINEERING CORRECTIONS FROM THE FEASIBILITY REVIEW
These are particularly important when studying the project.
1. ±24 V is not a universal op-amp supply
It can be a system rail.
Individual ICs may require:
	•	±15 V,
	•	±12 V,
	•	±18 V,
	•	or another suitable supply.

2. +24 dBu should not be interpreted as "every IC handles +24 dBu"
+24 dBu is:
34.7 Vpp
Only selected stages may need to swing this much.
Other circuits can operate at reduced internal levels.

3. RIAA RC calculations were not a finished RIAA circuit
The values were useful for understanding the time constants.
They do not constitute the final schematic.

4. 0–5 V CV is not compulsory inside every analogue stage
It is a useful control interface.
Internal circuits may instead use:
	•	currents,
	•	logarithmic conversion,
	•	dB/V relationships,
	•	device-specific control characteristics.

5. −20 dB pedal pad is not a final value
It was a prototype starting point.
The actual pedal population should influence the final interface.

6. −90 dBu is not yet a guaranteed full-mixer specification
A complete stage-by-stage noise budget is required.

7. 8 Hz–35 kHz is a target, not an assumed result
The final design must be measured.

8. Five-way KILL cannot simply be five unrelated filters
The bands need to be designed as a coherent filter bank.

9. The state-variable filter is not solved yet
The architecture is credible.
The actual:
	•	OTA,
	•	integrator,
	•	frequency converter,
	•	resonance loop,
	•	stability compensation,
still need to be designed.

39. CURRENT BOTTOM LINE
The project is feasible.
The current concept is coherent enough to move from architectural design into genuine analogue circuit engineering.
The main challenge is not that the concept is impossible. The main challenge is that several sections are complex enough that they now need proper circuit derivation instead of additional plausible-looking assumptions.
The most important distinction going forward is:
WHAT YOU HAVE DECIDED versus WHAT WAS PROPOSED AS AN ENGINEERING STARTING POINT
The current project is strongest when those two are kept separate.
The next major engineering step should therefore be the actual topology and derivation of the critical analogue blocks, rather than adding more features or provisional component values.
