1. Executive status
The overall system is technically feasible as a high-end custom analogue DJ / dub mixer. No major subsystem identified so far is
fundamentally impossible. The project is now moving from architecture into actual circuit engineering.
The main difficult blocks are the global state-variable filter, resonance/self-oscillation, the five-way KILL/filter bank, five-band creative EQ,
analogue CV distribution, the complete noise budget, power/ground architecture, and the modular backplane.
Engineering method from this point: requirement -> transfer function -> topology -> component values -> simulation -> PCB/layout ->
prototype -> measurement -> revision.

2. Global locked principles
• 100% analogue audio. No ADC, DAC, DSP or digital audio processing.
• Stereo master L/R. Mono sound-system operation is downstream.
• No pan or separate L/R user controls. Stereo controls are linked.
• Modular/serviceable construction. Functional cards/modules use defined interfaces; not necessarily literal Eurorack electrical standard.
• No hidden digital audio control.
• Extreme settings allowed for filtering, saturation and feedback, while electrical operation remains non-destructive.
• No intentional master compressor/limiter/clipping. Electrical protection is still required.
• No hot swapping.

Why OPA1654 for the phono front end
The MM cartridge is a high-impedance, inductive source. A FET-input audio amplifier is attractive at the cartridge interface because input
current noise is extremely low. TI specifies the OPA1652/1654 family at about 4.5 nV/sqrt(Hz) voltage noise at 1 kHz, 3 fA/sqrt(Hz) current
noise, 18 MHz GBW, unity-gain stability and operation up to +/-18 V. That makes the family a credible MM front-end choice. The OPA1612
remains a strong candidate for lower-impedance downstream stages, but its bipolar input makes it less attractive as the first MM device when
cartridge impedance rises with frequency.
RIAA topology choice
For this mixer I recommend the two-stage hybrid active/passive RIAA approach rather than putting the complete equalization into one
aggressive feedback loop. A real, measured MM implementation from audioXpress uses a JFET-input OPA1642, a first-stage gain of 21.3
dB, a passive 75 us section, a second stage implementing the 318 us and 3180 us constants, and about 42 dB total gain at 1 kHz. Its
reported measurement showed about +/-0.1 dB RIAA deviation from 20 Hz to 10 kHz. A Texas Instruments application report likewise
documents passive and active/passive RIAA topologies and emphasizes gain distribution for noise versus overload. These are references for
topology, not a claim that their exact values are already final for this mixer.
RIAA component policy
• Use 0.1% metal-film resistors in the RIAA network.
• Use low-DA film capacitors, preferably polypropylene, for the RIAA time-constant network; C0G/NP0 is appropriate for small-value RF/load
capacitors.
• Keep the 47 k ohm cartridge load physically at the RCA input connector.
• Keep the selectable cartridge capacitance physically close to the input connector.
• Keep the phono input area physically away from PSU switching nodes, control CVs, LEDs and headphone circuitry.
• Use a defined local phono signal-ground reference and a controlled chassis/earth connection rather than relying on arbitrary cable shields.
• No intentional 8-15 Hz rumble filter in Rev A; preserve the specified RIAA transfer and evaluate rumble filtering later as an optional function.

7. Global DJ filter system
Locked correction: frequency, resonance and overdrive are shared controls; each DJ channel only selects HPF / OFF / LPF.
• HPF: 20 Hz -> 1.5 kHz.
• LPF: 500 Hz -> 20 kHz.
• 24 dB/oct target.
• Candidate: 4-pole state-variable architecture, conceptually two cascaded 2-pole sections.
• 0-5 V is only a proposed control interface convention.
• Resonance: 0-70% normal, 70-90% aggressive, 90-100% self-oscillation.
• Proposed oscillation level: about +6 to +10 dBu, softly bounded.
• Overdrive follows filter/resonance and precedes the DJ meter; JFET then asymmetric-BJT prototypes.
• Filter mode switching: break-before-make with about 1-5 ms click suppression.
8. Five-way sound-system / isolator stage
DJ BUS -> ONE 5-WAY -> MASTER SUM. FX returns bypass the 5-way. External DSP performs the acoustic crossover.
• Physical KILL map: SUB ~20-90 Hz; KICK ~90-300/400 Hz; MID ~300/400 Hz-1.2 kHz; HIGH ~1.2-3.5 kHz; SUPER HI ~3.5-20 kHz.
• Creative ranges: 20-120 Hz, 80-400 Hz, 250 Hz-1.5 kHz, 800 Hz-5 kHz, 3-20 kHz.
• Max boosts: +15, +15, +15, +12, +9 dB respectively.
• Width target: narrow ~Q3, medium ~Q1, broad ~Q0.5.
• KILL must be designed as a coherent complementary filter bank and positioned so creative EQ cannot restore killed material.
• Five-way drive follows the EQ sum and precedes the 5-way meter.

20. Current bottom line
The project remains feasible. The phono section can now be treated as a concrete engineering work package rather than only a functional
requirement. The current Rev A direction is MM-only, 47 k ohm load, 100 pF default with internal alternatives, JFET-input op-amp front end,
approximately 21.3 dB first gain, hybrid active/passive RIAA, and approximately 40-42 dB total gain at 1 kHz.
Next engineering target: once the four phono questions are answered, freeze the actual RIAA topology and calculate the complete
component network, then move to the DJ EQ or global filter.
